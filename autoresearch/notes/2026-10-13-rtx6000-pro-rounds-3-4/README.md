# RTX PRO 6000 Blackwell: autoresearch rounds 3 and 4

These rounds extend [rounds 1–2](../2026-10-13-rtx6000-pro-two-rounds/README.md) on one RTX PRO 6000 Blackwell Server Edition (96 GiB nominal, 188 SMs, CUDA 12.8, driver 595.91.07) with a large-memory host. The production circuit registry uses 70 queries and 26 PoW bits. Inputs are four real one-block PIEs from the saved contiguous settlement campaign and 16 real, verified contiguous leaf proofs. The fixed Cairo coefficients are the authenticated 2.17 GB artifact with SHA-256 `4d4fda06dfa3bca19554510a158f6c50abad06a74d29c17885ed4cbb88ada34d`. Every completed proof was independently verified by the product before publication. These are local diagnostic timings, not ranked challenge receipts.

The unmodified round-3 baseline was `dd1cf227a6a594ad3f34a6e108709ac52a52d2ed`; the accepted implementation is `792ba78f112b3225fcd3eb4a403102acb3217e08`. The final `circuit-recursion-cuda-resident` ReleaseFast SM 120 build succeeded. Its executable SHA-256 was `73a0fc89a2ce142f44c3569e215a3d3f8ab2dbf4c450270746a5faa7a435ae77`. The remote measurement wrapper reports `source_commit=unknown` for the uncommitted worktree used during these runs; the two candidate source files on that host had identical hashes to the files in the accepted commit.

## Round 3: find the physical-memory boundary

The hypothesis was that, after commitment, moving the main and interaction evaluation arrays to host memory would free device pages for the saturated PIE. That opt-in experiment proved the **same bytes**, but it was slower. The canonical OODS and quotient stages still scan these arrays, so cold-prefetching them before those stages merely moved their cost into managed-memory faults and transfers. This experiment was rejected and its implementation removed.

| Saturated PIE `16309799_16309799` | Full Cairo + leaf wrap | Cairo proof/decode | OODS device interval | Quotient device interval | Requested managed D2H |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Existing phase-aware prefetch | 28.56 s | 21.59 s | 0.21 s | 4.26 s | 48.17 GB |
| Extra cold-trace prefetch | 39.87 s | 33.02 s | 4.08 s | 10.16 s | 107.73 GB |

The added 59.56 GB hostward request increased total time by 11.31 seconds. The direct result rules out simply parking committed evaluations on the CPU before OODS/quotient. It does not rule out a redesigned tiled commitment, quotient, and opening schedule. The large slots include a 31.87 GB lookup slab, 32.61 GB main evaluation array, 26.95 GB interaction evaluation array, and 16.30 GB main coefficients; the planner already aliases several of these when their lifetimes do not overlap. A future out-of-core path must account for every consumer of a tile and use a physical backing policy matched to those lifetimes.

We also proved `16309353_16309353`, the previously untested case between the fitting and oversubscribed examples, **without managed allocation**. Its 97.920 GB planned arena completed in 7.04 seconds, with 94,683 MiB sampled GPU peak and 2,570 MiB minimum reported free. The proof field, circuit hash, and preprocessed root match the saved H200 leaf exactly. The same host's CUDA device reports 101.976 GB total memory to the runtime. Strict allocation rejected the 103.123 and 118.891 GB cases at arena admission with `InsufficientDeviceMemory`; both subsequently proved with the admitted managed path. The 118.891 GB plan exceeds runtime-reported total by 16.915 GB. The observed strict-fit boundary is therefore between 97.920 and 103.123 GB planned, not a general guarantee for every geometry in that interval.

Of 41 one-block PIEs in the settlement metrics, the planned arena median is 90.538 GB and two plans exceed the runtime-reported device capacity. This is a useful admission screen, but only four one-block cases were proved in these RTX rounds. Sampling at roughly 250 ms can miss short GPU peaks, and the oversubscribed runs sampled only 4 MiB free. Such runs should not share the device with unrelated jobs.

## Round 4: remove PoW contention and budget fixed-data residency

Nsight Systems showed that the two PoW searches in one real two-leaf fold occupied 90.46 ms, or 27.3% of measured GPU kernel time. Each candidate was doing a global atomic read of the current best nonce. The CUDA kernel now refreshes that bound every 32 candidates and caches the proof's field-reduction setting. A stale bound can do extra hashing but cannot skip a smaller candidate; `atomicMin` still chooses the same minimum nonce. On the identical verified two-leaf fold, PoW kernel time fell to **6.03 ms (15.0× faster)** and its GPU-kernel share to 2.4%. The complete root proof hash was unchanged. The Metal-specific search path is untouched.

The GPU primitive gain is materially larger than the command gain because circuit construction, transforms, and serialization remain. The two 16-leaf full-command measurements with the new kernel were 9.56 and 9.90 seconds, versus 10.23 and 10.49 seconds before it (about 6% lower medians). All 15 reductions, including the root, were proved and verified on CUDA; the root proof was byte-for-byte identical. Sampled GPU peak stayed at 27,481 MiB. Smaller trees show fixed setup cost: the new 2-, 4-, and 8-leaf commands took 2.29, 3.41, and 5.29 seconds; prior measurements were 2.00, 3.13, and 5.46 seconds. The leaf-wrap phase stayed at roughly 1.3–1.6 seconds, so this work does **not** establish a material leaf-wrap wall-time win.

The second change addresses batch capacity. An authenticated fixed-coefficient device image occupies 2.172 GB outside the Cairo arena. Retaining it is useful for ordinary fitting PIEs, but it competes with an oversubscribed arena for physical pages. By default the app now releases an existing image, or skips creating one, when the planned arena plus image and the existing 256 MiB safety reserve exceed CUDA's reported total memory. `STWO_CAIRO_CUDA_STATIC_IMAGE=1` remains an explicit override for A/B runs; `=0` still disables images outright. On the fitting first leaf, sampled steady GPU use was about 92,953 MiB with the image and 90,873 MiB without it, a 2,080 MiB difference consistent with the 2.172 GB allocation. Before the later saturated leaf, the automatic policy evicted the image. Its complete proof still matched the saved H200 leaf exactly.

This is **persistent-allocation reduction**, not a claim that the saturated run's `nvidia-smi` peak fell: both policies filled the card to 97,249 MiB as managed pages migrated. Three auto/image-on comparisons were 27.21/27.22, 31.02/28.02, and 28.67/31.44 seconds for the full fitting→saturated batch. The last two pairs used unique output paths. The direction changes between pairs, so there is no reliable speed improvement from the image policy in this sample. The automatic choice protects memory headroom without a demonstrated median command penalty. A final build with the capacity formula repeated the fitting→saturated proof with the image evicted before the second leaf; both proofs still matched their references. A separate strict fitting→boundary batch kept the image for both leaves, proved both, and peaked at 96,731 MiB with 522 MiB sampled free. Per-leaf ingress, proof/decode, image-hit status, and wrap times are retained in [batch_trials.csv](batch_trials.csv).

## Capacity and next architecture test

The PoW fix removes a clear compute bottleneck in recursive proofs. It does not solve the saturated PIE's 16.915 GB virtual-arena excess over runtime-reported device capacity. The previous phase-aware managed path makes that PIE provable, but 19–21 seconds of Cairo proof/decode versus about 1 second for a fitting PIE is the current capacity tax. A genuinely strict-memory design needs to reduce the overlapping evaluation/coefficient live set, not just migrate the same bytes more often. The most promising next experiment is a bounded tile schedule that commits, evaluates, and later reconstructs only the verifier-requested openings, with exact proof parity and whole-device peak as gates. The rejected cold-prefetch run is evidence that a naïve after-commit eviction is too early. A CUDA DSL or a transform-kernel rewrite could help the **fitting** compute path once profiled, but neither changes the demonstrated capacity shortfall by itself.
